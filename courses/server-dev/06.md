# Onboarding and welcoming
The first minutes decide whether people stay. Make them easy.

**Discord's built-in tools (need Community on)**
- **Rules screening:** members read and accept the rules before they can talk
- **Server Guide / Onboarding:** ask a few questions when someone joins and give roles or channels based on their answers
- **Default channels:** the channels every new member sees first
- **Welcome screen:** a short intro with a few recommended channels

**Good onboarding questions**
- "What brings you here?" (support, buying, community)
- "Which service are you interested in?" (maps to a role or channel)
- "Do you want event pings?" (optional ping role)

Keep it to **3 or 4 questions**. Every extra step loses people.

**Welcome channel and message**
A short message with: who you are, where the rules are, where to get help, and one thing to do next.
```
Welcome to the server!
1. Read #rules
2. Say hi in #general
3. Need help? Open a ticket in #open-a-ticket
```
**Rules that work**
- 5 to 10 rules, short and plain
- Say the consequences (warn, mute, ban)
- Include "staff may moderate at their discretion"
- Number them, so mods can say "Rule 3"

**Self-assign roles:** buttons or dropdowns for ping roles and interests (a bot or Discord's onboarding can do this).

**Make the first message easy:** a #introductions channel with a prompt ("Tell us your name and what you're working on").

**Avoid:** walls of text, forced long verification, and sending new members to 12 different channels at once.

**Measure it:** Server Insights shows how many new members stay and talk. If few talk, simplify your first steps.

Checkpoint: invite a friend with a fresh account and watch what they do. Where do they get stuck?
