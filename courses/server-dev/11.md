# Managing your staff team
A server is only as good as its team.

**Recruiting**
- Look for members who are **helpful, patient and active**, not the loudest
- Ask for a short application: why, timezone, past experience, scenario questions
- Start people as **trial staff** or **Helper** with limited permissions
- Never promote someone just because they asked or are your friend

**Onboarding new staff**
- A **staff handbook**: rules, punishment ladder, templates, who to ask
- A private **#staff-chat** and **#staff-commands**
- A quick walkthrough of the tools: bot commands, ticket claim, logs
- A buddy: an experienced mod to shadow for the first week

**Clear roles**
- **Helper:** answers questions, reports problems
- **Moderator:** warns, times out, handles reports
- **Admin:** settings, roles, bots
- **Owner:** final say, high-risk decisions

**Keep staff active**
- Simple **activity checks** (once a month)
- A way to mark **leave of absence**
- Remove inactive staff politely: their permissions are a security risk

**Handling problems**
- Private feedback first, public praise often
- Handle staff disputes in a private channel, never in front of members
- If a mod abuses powers: remove their permissions first, investigate second
- Keep an **audit trail** (logs and written reasons)

**Security for staff**
- 2FA required
- No sharing accounts
- Never click suspicious links, even from "other staff" (accounts get hacked)
- Don't give people higher roles than they need

**Culture:** thank your team, hold short regular meetings and let them suggest improvements. Burned-out mods quit; appreciated ones stay for years.

Checkpoint: write a one-page staff handbook with your punishment ladder and ticket rules.
